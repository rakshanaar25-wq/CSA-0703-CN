Scanning the Wi-Fi networks around me showed a clear range of signal strengths, from around -40 dBm (very strong, close networks) down to -80 dBm (weak, distant ones). I noticed several networks were clustered on the same or overlapping channels (especially in the crowded 2.4GHz band), which can cause interference and slower speeds when many devices compete for the same airtime — this is similar to the collision problem I saw with hubs in the LAN task. Most networks I found were using 802.11n or 802.11ac; [note if you found any ax networks]. In a crowded place like a hostel or classroom with many devices connected to the same access point, Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax) would help because it uses OFDMA to let the router talk to multiple devices at once in smaller time slices, rather than one at a time, and it handles high-density environments with far less congestion and better battery efficiency for connected devices.
